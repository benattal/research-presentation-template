O'Toole, Raskar, and Kutulakos built the probing equation into hardware.

- Click 1, prototype: the scene is lit by a projector through a beam splitter and viewed by a camera through relay lenses, with an LCD panel in the image plane. During one exposure, a sequence of projector patterns (primal codes) and a matching sequence of LCD masks on the camera pixels (dual codes) change in lockstep. The photo is $(\Pi \odot \mathbf{T})\mathbf{1}$, with the probing matrix $\Pi$ set by the two code sequences.
- Click 2: the scene under white light.
- Click 3: one raw photo with codes chosen to block the direct paths. Only global light remains. The objects embedded inside the wax disk appear, because their light reaches the camera only by scattering inside the wax.

To remember: the element-wise product with $\mathbf{T}$ happens in the optics, and $\mathbf{T}$ itself is never measured.
