Kajiya's rendering equation, one term per step, at a point $\mathbf{x}$ on the floor of the lamp-lit room. Points are bold, $\mathbf{x}$; plain $x$ stays the radiance vector of the Neumann series section. Each term's picture is drawn in its term's color.

- **Outgoing.** $L_o(\mathbf{x}, \omega_o)$ is the radiance leaving $\mathbf{x}$ in direction $\omega_o$: the white ray. Here $\omega_o$ points toward the camera of the later slides.
- **Emitted.** $L_e$ is the radiance a surface emits. Only the lamp emits in this room (its light is drawn leaving it). At the floor point $L_e(\mathbf{x}, \omega_o) = 0$, so all the light leaving $\mathbf{x}$ is reflected light.
- **Reflected.** The integral over the hemisphere $H^2$ of directions $\omega_i$ (in flatland, a half circle): the incident radiance $L_i(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i)$, weighted by the BRDF $f_r(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i, \omega_o)$ and the cosine of the incidence angle $\theta_i$. Each ray has the color of the light arriving along it: bright from the lamp, red from the left wall. The dashed circle is the cosine weight. The floor is diffuse, so $f_r$ is a constant (its albedo divided by 2 in flatland, the counterpart of albedo$/\pi$ in 3D), and grazing light contributes little.
- **Ray tracing.** The incident radiance is not a new unknown. Radiance is constant along a ray in vacuum, so the light arriving at $\mathbf{x}$ from direction $\omega_i$ is the light leaving the first surface point on that ray, $r(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i)$, in the direction back toward $\mathbf{x}$, $-\omega_i$. $r$ is the ray-tracing function. The ray is traced from $\mathbf{x}$ to the ceiling, and the light leaving the ceiling there returns along it.
- **Propagation and scattering.** Substitute the ray-tracing relation into the integral: only outgoing radiance is left, on both sides, so we drop the subscript $o$. Two operators appear. $G$ is propagation, ray tracing in every direction: $(GL)(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i) = L(r(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i), -\omega_i)$, read at the dots on the surfaces. Visibility is part of $G$. $S$ is scattering: the integral with the BRDF and the cosine, the lobe. The animation applies them in this order: every direction is traced, the light of the surfaces reached arrives at $\mathbf{x}$, and the lobe weights it. The unknown appears both outside and inside an integral: a Fredholm equation of the second kind.
- **Discretized.** Split the surfaces into patches (the ticks) and the directions into 16 bins (the spokes). Radiance becomes a vector, and the equation is the fixed-point equation of the Neumann series section, $x = e + Ax$, with $x = L$, $e = L_e$, and $A = SG$ one bounce: propagate, then scatter.

The weighted sum of the incident light equals the outgoing light of the solve of $x = e + Ax$ at the point (the deck's tests check it on every diffuse patch).

To remember: the rendering equation is $x = e + Ax$, with $A$ one bounce of light.
