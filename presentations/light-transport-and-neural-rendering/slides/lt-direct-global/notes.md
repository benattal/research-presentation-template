Nayar et al.'s separation of direct and global light.

Project a high-frequency pattern, such as a checkerboard with half the pixels on, and shift it. A camera pixel receives direct light only when its projector pixel is on. Global light (interreflections, subsurface scattering) arrives from many projector pixels, so it is about half its full value under any shifted pattern. Over the shifts, the maximum at a pixel is the direct light plus half the global light, and the minimum is half the global light. So $L_d = L_{max} - L_{min}$ and $L_g = 2 L_{min}$.

- Eggs: the direct component has sharp shadows; the global component is the light the eggs bounce onto each other.
- Peppers: almost all their light is global, from subsurface scattering. The direct component is the specular highlights.

To remember: with high spatial frequencies in the illumination, light that bounced once is separable from light that bounced more, from a few photos and with no measured $T$.
