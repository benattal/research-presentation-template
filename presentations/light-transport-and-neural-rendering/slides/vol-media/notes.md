Transition: a volume that absorbs, scatters, or emits light is called a participating medium. Four examples.

- Fog and clouds: water droplets scatter light. The shafts in the fog are light scattered toward the camera from the sunlit parts of the air.
- Smoke: soot mostly absorbs light, so the plume is dark.
- Jade: a solid can be a medium too. Light enters the stone, scatters inside, and leaves elsewhere, which gives the soft, glowing look. This is subsurface scattering; skin, marble, wax, and milk behave the same way.

The sentence to remember: in a medium, light can be absorbed, scattered, or emitted at every point along a ray, so radiance changes continuously along the ray.

The question it invites: the cloud and the smoke are both small particles in air, so why is one white and the other dark? Soot absorbs most of the light that meets it; a water droplet scatters almost all of it. In a cloud, light scatters many times and leaves in every direction, nearly all of it still there, so the cloud is white. In smoke, light is absorbed before it gets out.
