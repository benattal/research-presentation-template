Structured light (projecting stripes and triangulating) assumes direct light, and it fails under indirect light.

Row 1: a mannequin head beside a mirror. With conventional projection, the stripes reflected by the mirror land on the face as a second set of fringes, and the 3D reconstruction fails. With indirect-invariant imaging, the same patterns are projected but the indirect paths are blocked by probing, and the same algorithm recovers the face, even its hidden side through the mirror.

Row 2: a bowl and an open book, with strong diffuse and specular interreflections. The conventional scan is corrupted inside the bowl and between the pages; the probed scan is clean.

To remember: with probing, existing 3D scanning algorithms work in scenes with indirect light, unchanged.
