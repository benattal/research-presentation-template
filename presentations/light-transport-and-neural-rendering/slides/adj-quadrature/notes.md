Transition: the adjoint idea, applied to NeRF's quadrature on one ray: the same cloud and ray as on the quadrature slide.

- Step 1, the forward march: the samples are composited front to back, $\hat C = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i c_i$ with $T_i = e^{-\sum_{j<i} \sigma_j \delta_j}$. The bars are the terms $T_i\alpha_i c_i$, in each sample's color; their sum is the pixel.
- Step 2, differentiate with respect to one density $\sigma_i$. Two things change. Sample $i$ itself becomes more opaque, which adds $\delta_i T_{i+1} c_i$ (since $\partial \alpha_i / \partial \sigma_i = \delta_i e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}$ and $T_{i+1} = T_i e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}$). And every sample behind it is dimmed: each $T_k$ with $k > i$ loses a fraction $\delta_i$, which removes $\delta_i$ times the color behind sample $i$, $\hat C - \hat C_{\le i} = \sum_{k > i} T_k \alpha_k c_k$. The blue bars are $\partial \hat C / \partial \sigma_i$ (luminance): negative in front of the bright surface, where more density hides it, and positive just at the surface.
- In front of the surface the bars are equal. With equal spacing $\delta$, consecutive bars differ by $\delta\, T_{i+1}\,(c_{i+1} - c_i)$, which is zero where the color is constant.
- Step 3, the second march: the color behind sample $i$ is the final color $\hat C$ minus the running sum $\hat C_{\le i}$, accumulated in a second march from the front; each sample's gradient is computed on the way, so nothing is stored per sample, and memory is constant in the number of samples. The black staircase is the color behind each sample, the running sum of the terms from the back, drawn as the march passes. Multiplying by $\partial \ell / \partial \hat C$, the gradient of the loss with respect to the pixel, gives the gradient of the loss.

The same derivative read as the adjoint method: $\partial \hat C / \partial \sigma_i = \delta_i\, T_{i+1}\, (c_i - L_{i+1})$, with $L_{i+1} = (\hat C - \hat C_{\le i}) / T_{i+1}$ the light arriving at sample $i$ from behind (the quadrature restarted after sample $i$). $T_{i+1}$, the transmittance from there to the camera, plays the role of the importance $y$, and $c_i - L_{i+1}$ the role of $(\partial_\theta A)\, x$: with a larger $\sigma_i$, more of $c_i$ is emitted there and more of $L_{i+1}$ is absorbed.

The sentence to remember: the backward pass needs only the final color and a second march, not a record of the forward pass. It is the adjoint method on a single ray without scattering.

Check: the formula agrees with central finite differences of $\hat C$ for every sample, and so do the adjoint form and the difference between consecutive bars (the deck's volume tests). By hand, for one sample, $\hat C = (1 - e^{-\sigma\delta}) c$ and the formula gives $\delta e^{-\sigma\delta} c$, the derivative.
